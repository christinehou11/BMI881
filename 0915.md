In the paper, John Snow demonstrated that cholera is the gastrointestinal infection disease that the contaminated materials enter the alimentary canal, specically through polluted water. Snow utilized many examples to describe that the pathology starts from the inside alimentary canal, where severe loss of fluid from the stomach and intestines leads to dehydration and circulatory collapse. He mentioned many examples, including household investigations, case records, and observations on cholera cases. The primary discussion focuses on the London outbreaks, and he lists out the sewage-contanimated water souces, such as the water pump and river intakes, alongwith the transmission in overcrowded surroundings. 

What I found most interesting about this paper is how Snow builds the connection between gastrointestinal pathology and the social behaviors such as the environment maintainance and human life styles. Cholera, to some extent, reflects many public health problems such as sanitaion, water maintainance, population density, and fecal-oral interactions. Many current digestive illness like E.coli infections are somewhat depending on host's exposure to surroundings, digestion, and frequent sanitation. What's more, I recognize how the public health, environmental policy, and socioeconomic devlopment are involved together. Snow mentioned that the households are supplied by different water companies, which highlights the public disparities. The access to cleaner water seems to be tied with consumer choice and technology. This further suggests that environmental protection policies and clean infrasturcture build-up are essential to ensure public health.
