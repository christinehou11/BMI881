## 1124

The author discusses how people learn from data. He proposes "Greater Data Science", which is a broader definition of data science field. This definition includes data preparation, data exploration, data representation, computing, modeling, result, and conclusion. He points out that machine learning's biggest secret to succeed is the Common Task Framework—giving competitors the same dataset and an objective way to score results. He mainly proposes to treat the analysis process as a separate research work. When people share the data and codes, other researchers are able to use other different methods to analyze the same dataset, or evalaute one specific method across many independent studies. These experiments could help to justify whether one finding is reasonable, and also help to identify specific assumptions made during analysis.

It is surprising that simple, basic models perform similar to complex machine learning algorithms in real-world projects. His argument that traditional statistics programs miss out by ignoring the Common Task Framework is also very interesting. However, Donoho seems to ignore the usage of big data engineering tools, which these computing technology can help a lot to shape the questions we actually can ask. What's more, his prediction for future is a little bit over-optimistic and idealistic to me because I think there are many real-world difficulties such as privacy laws, corporate secrets, and academic competitions.
