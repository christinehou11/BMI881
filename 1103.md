## 1103
The authors evaluated on how often the standard fMRI analysese detect the brain activation under task-free performance. They used resting-state fMRI data from 499 healthy controls to conduct task group analyses by three popular software packages. At a 5% familywise error rate, about 5 out of every 100 analyses should identify at least one significant result by chance. They found that voxelwise inference is conservative but clusterwise inference is invalid, that at least one false positive in up to 70% anlayses for clusterwise inference. The authors conducted the spatial correlation analysis and tried to find the discrepancy that extended farher than the models assumed. The permutation tests made the error rate approximately 5% in two-group comparisons, although their performance was less consitsent in one-group analyses.

I found the difference between clusterwise and voxelwise results interesting. The paper shows tht the noise can extend across region of the brain. If a model underestimate now far that noise extends, a cluster can look unusually large even when participants have performed no task. I also like the authors' decision to test this using real resting-state scans. Using real scans allowed the authors to see how often noise alone produced a large cluster. The simulation based on the software's assumptions could have missed the problem because it might generate fewer larger clusters of noise than actually occur in brain scans.
