## 1020

Wang et.al developed the risk prediction model using deep learning algorithm to diagnose nonmelanoma skin cancer (NMSC). The prediction model uses multidimensiona, nonimgaing electronic medical records (EMR) from the Taiwan National Helath Insurance Research Database. The data was collected from 1829 patients and 7665 controls without disease that the period of time covers three years. The sequential medical events are converted into the temporal image-like matrices, and are fed into a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to predict the disease risk over the following years. The study create the features using age, sex, disgnostic codes, and some related medical information. The study results reflect that some past diagnoses such as carcinoma in situ of skin, chronic comorbidities, and prescriptions for specific medications served as top discriminative features. The conclusion is that the deep learning algorithm can help to stratify the skin cancer risk effectively such that the high-risk populations can be targeted without relying on traditional clinical methods such as direct UV exposure or physical skin examinations.

The paper's topic is very interesting. Using the creative structuring of longitudinal EMR data into matrices, allowing for CNN to analyze the diagnostic and durg histories. It is impressive to see that the nonimaging administrative features can have better performance than traditional cancer prediction tools. The authors mentioned that some drugs with photosensitizing properties and some chronic conditions may be markers of aging and healthcare use, but the deep learning algorithms could mistakenly identify some unmeasured confounding variables as risk drivers. What's more, the study explicitly meausured the data from Asian cohort with somehow low baseline cancer incidence and lacked lifestyle confounders like sun exposure habits or genetics, it could raise questions about how well the model and its predictive features would generalize to other populations.

## Questions
1. The CNN algorithm was trained on Asian cohort from Taiwan, do we need to build extra validations or adjustments to generalize this tool?

2. Patients with more healthcare visits have more recorded diagnoses and prescription. How could we distinguish cancer risk from differences in healthcare use and detection?
