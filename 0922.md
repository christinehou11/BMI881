## 0922

The study described in the paper is the "Lipid Research Clinics Coronary Primary Prevention Trial (LRC-CPPT)". The authors evaluated whether lowering total plasma cholesterol and low-density lipoprortein cholesterol (LDL-C) could reduce the risk of coronary heart disease (CHD) among 3806 middle-aged white men with high cholesterol over an average follow-up period of 7.4 years. Both treatment and control groups had the moderate cholesterol-lowering diet, while the treatment group received cholestyramine and the control group received the placebo. The treatment group had greater reductions in total cholesterol and LDL-C. This lipid lowering drove the significant reduction in the endpoint of definite CHD death or nonfatal myocardial infaction. The treament group also had lower rates of angina, positive exercise stress tests, and coronary bypass surgery. Other analyses suggested the dose-response relationship in which greater cholesterol reductions were associated with greater reductions in CHD risk.

The study presents strong evidence for hypothesis testing, but I was very surprised by the the excess of violent and accidental deaths such as homocides, suicides, and traffic accidents in the treatment group. This is the finding/result I never expected to see before reading this paper. What's more, I feel concerned about generalizability of the study because the study focused on only middle-aged white males. As the end of this paper commented on, a more conclusive or comprehensive study may include participantes of different sexes, ethnicities, or residential areas. I would also be interested in testing whether the residential areas is related to the CHD risk.

Questions:
1. How convincing is the excess of accidental and violent deaths happened by chance? What would some guidelines be made to deal with these unexpected situations? How should researchers interpret and report the unexpected outcomes like these?
2. The authors mentioned that the adhenrence to drug seems to be a heavily compromised side effect. In real practices, how could the healthcare center or clinical institutions balance the efficacy of medication with the long-term prescriptions?
